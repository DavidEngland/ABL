§X. Sturm–Liouville Structure of Businger–Dyer Similarity Functions

The Businger–Dyer similarity functions for momentum and heat exhibit power‑law behavior near neutral stability. We show that these exponents correspond exactly to the boundary behavior of eigenfunctions of a one‑parameter family of ultraspherical Sturm–Liouville operators. This provides a unified spectral framework for momentum, heat, and passive‑scalar similarity functions.

---

1. Ultraspherical Operator and Eigenbasis

For \(\lambda > -\frac12\), define the ultraspherical Sturm–Liouville operator

\mathcal{L}_\lambda[y]
= -\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[(1-x^2)^{\lambda+\frac12} y^\prime(x)\right]
+ \lambda(\lambda+1)(1-x^2)^{\lambda-\frac12} y(x),


with weight

w_\lambda(x) = (1-x^2)^{\lambda-\frac12}.


The eigenfunctions are Gegenbauer polynomials:

\mathcal{L}_\lambda[C_n^{(\lambda)}] = n(n+2\lambda)\, C_n^{(\lambda)}.


---

2. Pullback to MOST Stability Variable

MOST uses

\zeta = z/L, \qquad \zeta<0 \text{ in unstable flow}.


Introduce

x = \sqrt{-b\zeta}, \qquad x\in (0,1).


The pullback of \(\mathcal{L}_\lambda\) to \(\zeta\) yields

\mathcal{L}_\lambda^\zeta[y]
= -\frac{d}{d\zeta}\!\left[p_\lambda(\zeta)\, y^\prime(\zeta)\right]
+ q_\lambda(\zeta)\, y(\zeta),


with

p_\lambda(\zeta) = (1+b\zeta)^{\lambda+\frac12} |\zeta|^{-1/2},
\qquad
w_\lambda(\zeta) = (1+b\zeta)^{\lambda-\frac12} |\zeta|^{-1/2}.


The singularity at \(\zeta=0\) corresponds to the ultraspherical endpoint \(x=0\).

3. Mapping Businger–Dyer Exponents to Ultraspherical Index

The Businger–Dyer similarity functions satisfy

\phi(\zeta) \sim (1 - b\zeta)^{-\alpha}, \qquad \zeta\to 0^-.


The ultraspherical generating function behaves as

(1 - b\zeta)^{-\lambda}.


Thus

{\lambda = \alpha.}


This identifies the empirical exponents as spectral parameters.

Theorem 1 (Ultraspherical Representation of MOST Similarity Functions)

Let \(\phi(\zeta)\) satisfy

\phi(\zeta) \sim (1 - b\zeta)^{-\alpha}.


Define \(x=\sqrt{-b\zeta}\). Then:

1. \(u(x)=\phi(\zeta(x))\) lies in the domain of \(\mathcal{L}_\lambda\) with \(\lambda=\alpha\).

3. The induced operator in \(\zeta\) is\mathcal{L}_\lambda^\zeta[y]

II = -\frac12 b_{ij}b_{ij}, \qquad III = \frac13 b_{ij}b_{jk}b_{ki}.

This provides a conformal‑like spectral embedding of the Lumley triangle.

Appendix A. Operator Domain, Self‑Adjointness, and Endpoint Classification

This appendix gives the formal Sturm–Liouville machinery.

---

A.1. Domain

Define

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_\lambda)
= \left\{ y\in AC[-1,1] : (1-x^2)^{\lambda+\frac12} y^\prime\in AC[-1,1],\; \mathcal{L}_\lambda[y]\in L^2(w_\lambda)\right\}.


---

A.2. Endpoint Classification

At \(x=\pm1\):

• The weight behaves as \((1-x^2)^{\lambda-\frac12}\).
• For \(\lambda>0\), both endpoints are limit‑point.
• Thus no boundary conditions are required for self‑adjointness.


---

A.3. Self‑Adjointness

The operator is self‑adjoint on \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_\lambda)\) because:

1. Both endpoints are limit‑point.
2. The differential expression is formally symmetric.
3. The maximal and minimal operators coincide.


---

A.4. Spectrum

The spectrum is purely discrete:

\sigma(\mathcal{L}_\lambda)=\{n(n+2\lambda)\}_{n=0}^\infty.


Eigenfunctions form a complete orthogonal basis in \(L^2((-1,1),w_\lambda)\).
