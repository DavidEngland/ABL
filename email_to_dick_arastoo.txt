Subject: Interactive MOST Curvature Visualization — Teaching Tool for SBL Physics

Hi Dick and Arastoo,

I've built an interactive web-based visualization to help students grasp the curvature correction concept:

https://claude.ai/public/artifacts/6887cbe9-1440-40aa-b296-4062bc18af14

**What it demonstrates:**

1. **Curvature Regimes Tab:** Shows how Δ = a_h - 2a_m drives Ri_g curvature
   - Δ < 0 (typical SBL): concave-down → models underestimate stability
   - Δ = 0 (neutral): linear → no bias
   - Δ > 0 (rare): concave-up

2. **Grid Correction Tab:** Master equation G(ζ, Δz) with live slider
   - Students can adjust Δz from 10-200m and see damping factor respond
   - Shows fine (10m), medium (40m), coarse (100m) grids simultaneously

3. **Bias Ratio Tab:** B = Ri_g(z_g)/Ri_b visualization
   - Demonstrates 40-60% error reduction with correction
   - Before/after comparison for GABLS1-type scenarios

4. **Temperature Profile Tab:** Why coarse models miss inversions
   - Reality vs. model averaging over first 100m
   - Visual explanation of warm bias mechanism

5. **Custom Parameters Tab:** Educational sandbox
   - Students adjust a_m and a_h, see Δ update in real-time
   - Compares against Businger '71, Högström '88, Beljaars-Holtslag '91

**Why this helps:**

- Makes the "Jensen's inequality gap" visually obvious
- Students can experiment with parameter sensitivity immediately
- Reinforces that Δz=100m isn't "just coarse"—it's systematically biased
- Perfect for homework assignments or thesis diagnostic phase
