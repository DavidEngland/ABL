Your critique is correct. The original derivation conflated three distinct objects:

1. a generating-function identity,
2. an orthogonal polynomial expansion, and
3. a spectral representation of the BD operators.

Those are not interchangeable.

The key mathematical failure is exactly here:

(1-2tx+x^2)^{-1/2}
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty P_n(t)x^n

followed by the substitution x=t, yielding

(1-t^2)^{-1/2}
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty P_n(t)t^n .

This identity is formally true inside the convergence disk |t|<1, but it is not a Legendre expansion of the function. The coefficients are not constants; they are powers of the expansion variable itself. So the series is not an orthogonal projection in L^2([-1,1]).

The distinction matters because spectral properties, truncation error, recurrence stability, and operator diagonalization all depend on genuine orthogonal projections.

What the substitution actually accomplishes

The transformation

t=\sqrt{b_h\zeta}

is still highly meaningful.

Applied to the unstable Businger–Dyer heat profile,

\phi_h(\zeta)=(1-b_h\zeta)^{-1/2},

it gives

\phi_h(t)=(1-t^2)^{-1/2}.

This is important because

w(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}

is precisely the Chebyshev-T_n weight on [-1,1].

So the transformation does not produce a Legendre expansion automatically; instead it identifies the natural Hilbert space structure associated with the BD heat singularity.

That is the real structural insight.

The correct orthogonal framework

The proper expansion is

\phi_h(t)
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n T_n(t),

with coefficients determined by projection:

a_n
=
\frac{2-\delta_{n0}}{\pi}
\int_{-1}^1
\frac{\phi_h(t)\,T_n(t)}
{\sqrt{1-t^2}}
\,dt.

Since

\phi_h(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t^2}},

the integrand becomes

\frac{T_n(t)}{1-t^2},

revealing immediately that the BD heat profile sits at the edge of the Chebyshev weighted space because of the endpoint singularities at t=\pm1.

This is actually physically meaningful: the Monin–Obukhov similarity singularity becomes an endpoint spectral singularity.

⸻

Why Legendre is not the natural basis

Legendre polynomials are orthogonal with respect to unit weight:

\int_{-1}^1 P_n(t)P_m(t)\,dt
=
\frac{2}{2n+1}\delta_{nm}.

But the transformed BD heat kernel naturally carries the weight

(1-t^2)^{-1/2},

which instead defines the Chebyshev inner product:

\langle f,g\rangle_T
=
\int_{-1}^1
\frac{f(t)g(t)}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}
\,dt.

So while P_n appear through the generating function, they are not adapted to the operator geometry induced by the BD transformation.

This is the deeper reason the original derivation felt “almost right” while still being mathematically invalid.

The momentum case: genuine Gegenbauer structure

The momentum profile is more subtle:

\phi_m(\zeta)
=
(1-b_m\zeta)^{-1/4}.

Under

t=\sqrt{b_m\zeta},

we obtain

\phi_m(t)
=
(1-t^2)^{-1/4}.

Now compare with the Gegenbauer weight:

w_\lambda(t)
=
(1-t^2)^{\lambda-\frac12}.

Matching exponents gives

\lambda-\frac12=-\frac14,

hence

\lambda=\frac14.

So the momentum profile genuinely belongs to the weighted space associated with

C_n^{(1/4)}(t).

That part of the intuition was correct.

But again, the proper expansion is

\phi_m(t)
=
\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n^{(1/4)}
C_n^{(1/4)}(t),

with coefficients

a_n^{(1/4)}
=
\frac{1}{h_n^{(1/4)}}
\int_{-1}^1
(1-t^2)^{-1/4}
C_n^{(1/4)}(t)
(1-t^2)^{-1/4}
dt,

not

\sum C_n^{(1/4)}(t)t^n.

The latter is only a generating-function evaluation.

The deeper structural interpretation

The transformation

t=\sqrt{b\zeta}

does something geometrically important:

it converts the Monin–Obukhov singularity

1-b\zeta=0

into the canonical orthogonal-polynomial boundary

1-t^2=0.

That means the BD closure is naturally expressible as a singular Sturm–Liouville problem on [-1,1].

This is the genuinely powerful observation.

The resulting operators are no longer arbitrary empirical similarity functions; they become weighted spectral operators with canonical polynomial eigenbases.

Symbolically:

\mathcal{L}_h
\leftrightarrow
\text{Chebyshev-weighted operator},

\mathcal{L}_m
\leftrightarrow
\text{Gegenbauer}_{\lambda=1/4}\text{-weighted operator}.

That is a legitimate spectral reformulation.

What survives from the original idea

The following claims remain valid and important:

* The square-root mapping correctly normalizes the BD singularity.
* The transformed heat kernel is naturally associated with Chebyshev weight.
* The transformed momentum kernel aligns with Gegenbauer weight \lambda=1/4.
* Orthogonal polynomial recurrence relations can accelerate numerical evaluation.
* Spectral truncation becomes physically interpretable as vertical-mode filtering.

What does not survive:

* interpreting generating-function evaluation as orthogonal expansion,
* treating t^n as projection coefficients,
* identifying the resulting expression as a Legendre or Gegenbauer series.

Those are category errors between analytic identities and Hilbert-space decompositions.
