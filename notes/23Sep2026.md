In Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) closure mappings, converting non-dimensional similarity derivatives $R^{(n)}(\zeta)$ into physical vertical spatial derivatives $\frac{d^m Ri}{dz^m}$ requires Faà di Bruno’s formula for higher-order chain rules. The transformation is governed by the **partial Bell matrix system**:

$$\mathbf{Ri}_z = \mathbf{B} \mathbf{R}_\zeta$$

$$\begin{bmatrix} Ri_z \\ Ri_{zz} \\ Ri_{zzz} \\ Ri_{zzzz} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} B_{1,1} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ B_{2,1} & B_{2,2} & 0 & 0 \\ B_{3,1} & B_{3,2} & B_{3,3} & 0 \\ B_{4,1} & B_{4,2} & B_{4,3} & B_{4,4} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R'(\zeta) \\ R''(\zeta) \\ R'''(\zeta) \\ R^{(4)}(\zeta) \end{bmatrix}$$

---

## 1. Recurrence Relations for Partial Bell Polynomials

The entries $B_{m,n} \equiv B_{m,n}\left(\zeta_z, \zeta_{zz}, \dots, \zeta_{(m-n+1)}\right)$ of the partial Bell matrix can be generated through two complementary recurrence relations.

### A. Summation Recurrence Relation (Faà di Bruno Identity)

The partial Bell polynomial entry for row $m+1$ and column $n$ is obtained by summing over prior rows $j$ from $n-1$ to $m$:

$$B_{m+1, n} = \sum_{j=n-1}^{m} \binom{m}{j} \left( \frac{d^{m+1-j}\zeta}{dz^{m+1-j}} \right) B_{j, n-1} \tag{Recurrence 1}$$

where $\binom{m}{j}$ is the binomial coefficient and $B_{0,0} = 1$. This matches the classic combinatorial definition of partial Bell polynomials.

### B. Differential Recurrence Relation (Subroutine-Friendly)

Differentiating the $m$-th derivative vector component with respect to $z$ yields an alternative differential recurrence that is ideal for numerical implementation:

$$B_{m+1, n} = \zeta_z B_{m, n-1} + \frac{d}{dz} B_{m, n} \tag{Recurrence 2}$$

with boundary conditions $B_{m,0} = 0$ and $B_{m, m+1} = 0$.

#### Step-by-Step Construction of $\mathbf{B}$:

* **Row 1 ($m=1$):**
$B_{1,1} = \zeta_z$
* **Row 2 ($m=2$):**
$B_{2,1} = \frac{d}{dz}(\zeta_z) = \zeta_{zz}$
$B_{2,2} = \zeta_z B_{1,1} = \zeta_z^2$
* **Row 3 ($m=3$):**
$B_{3,1} = \frac{d}{dz}(\zeta_{zz}) = \zeta_{zzz}$
$B_{3,2} = \zeta_z (\zeta_{zz}) + \frac{d}{dz}(\zeta_z^2) = \zeta_z \zeta_{zz} + 2\zeta_z \zeta_{zz} = 3\zeta_z \zeta_{zz}$
$B_{3,3} = \zeta_z (\zeta_z^2) = \zeta_z^3$
* **Row 4 ($m=4$):**
$B_{4,1} = \frac{d}{dz}(\zeta_{zzz}) = \zeta_{zzzz}$
$B_{4,2} = \zeta_z (\zeta_{zzz}) + \frac{d}{dz}(3\zeta_z \zeta_{zz}) = 4\zeta_z \zeta_{zzz} + 3\zeta_{zz}^2$
$B_{4,3} = \zeta_z (3\zeta_z \zeta_{zz}) + \frac{d}{dz}(\zeta_z^3) = 6\zeta_z^2 \zeta_{zz}$
$B_{4,4} = \zeta_z (\zeta_z^3) = \zeta_z^4$

## 2. Single-Coefficient Businger–Dyer Closure Mapping

Under the rational Businger–Dyer closure $R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta}{1 + \beta \zeta} = \frac{1}{\beta}\left(1 - \frac{1}{1 + \beta \zeta}\right)$, the non-dimensional derivative input vector is:

$$R^{(n)}(\zeta) = (-1)^{n-1} n! \, \beta^{n-1} (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-(n+1)} \qquad (n \ge 1) \tag{1}$$

Substituting into $\mathbf{Ri}_z = \mathbf{B} \mathbf{R}_\zeta$ and factoring out $(1 + \beta \zeta)^{-2}$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} Ri_z \\ Ri_{zz} \\ Ri_{zzz} \\ Ri_{zzzz} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \zeta_z & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^2 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zzz} & 3\zeta_z\zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^3 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zzzz} & 4\zeta_z\zeta_{zzz} + 3\zeta_{zz}^2 & 6\zeta_z^2\zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-2} \\ -2\beta (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-3} \\ 6\beta^2 (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-4} \\ -24\beta^3 (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-5} \end{bmatrix} \tag{2}$$

Evaluating row by row gives:

* **First Derivative ($Ri_z$):**
$$Ri_z = \frac{\zeta_z}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \tag{3}$$


* **Second Derivative ($Ri_{zz}$):**
$$Ri_{zz} = \frac{1}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \left[ \zeta_{zz} - \frac{2\beta \zeta_z^2}{1 + \beta \zeta} \right] \tag{4}$$


* **Third Derivative ($Ri_{zzz}$):**
$$Ri_{zzz} = \frac{1}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \left[ \zeta_{zzz} - \frac{6\beta \zeta_z \zeta_{zz}}{1 + \beta \zeta} + \frac{6\beta^2 \zeta_z^3}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \right] \tag{5}$$


* **Fourth Derivative ($Ri_{zzzz}$):**
$$Ri_{zzzz} = \frac{1}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \left[ \zeta_{zzzz} - \frac{2\beta (4\zeta_z \zeta_{zzz} + 3\zeta_{zz}^2)}{1 + \beta \zeta} + \frac{36\beta^2 \zeta_z^2 \zeta_{zz}}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} - \frac{24\beta^3 \zeta_z^4}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^3} \right] \tag{6}$$

## 3. Dual-Coefficient Monin–Obukhov Closure Mapping

For the dual-coefficient formulation $R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta (\alpha + \beta_h \zeta)}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^2}$ (where $\alpha = \text{Pr}_t(0)$ is the neutral turbulent Prandtl number and $\beta_h \neq \beta_m$), taking derivatives yields the vector $\mathbf{R}_\zeta$:

$$\mathbf{R}_\zeta = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\alpha + (2\beta_h - \alpha \beta_m)\zeta}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^3} \\ \frac{2 \left[ \beta_m (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)\zeta + (\beta_h - 2\alpha \beta_m) \right]}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^4} \\ \frac{-6 \left[ \beta_m^2 (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)\zeta + \beta_m (2\beta_h - 3\alpha \beta_m) \right]}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^5} \\ \frac{24 \left[ \beta_m^3 (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)\zeta + \beta_m^2 (3\beta_h - 4\alpha \beta_m) \right]}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^6} \end{bmatrix} \tag{7}$$

Multiplying $\mathbf{B} \mathbf{R}_\zeta$ incorporates separate heat ($\beta_h$) and momentum ($\beta_m$) expansion slopes into the physical spatial derivative components.

## 4. Asymptotic Limits & NWP Solver Implications

### A. Near-Neutral Limit ($\zeta \to 0$)

At neutrality ($\zeta \to 0$), denominator prefactors approach $1.0$, leaving spatial derivatives entirely unattenuated:

* **Single-Coefficient:** $R'(0) = 1, \quad R''(0) = -2\beta, \quad R'''(0) = 6\beta^2, \quad R^{(4)}(0) = -24\beta^3$
* **Dual-Coefficient:** $R'(0) = \alpha, \quad R''(0) = 2(\beta_h - 2\alpha \beta_m)$

Using standard operational constants ($\alpha = 1.0, \beta_m = 6.0, \beta_h = 5.0$):

* $R''(0) = 2(5 - 12) = -14$ for dual-coefficient vs. $R''(0) = -10$ for single-coefficient ($\beta = 5$).

**Physical & Numerical Consequences:**

1. **Initial Curvature Spike:** Dual-coefficient models induce a **~40% steeper initial curvature spike** near neutral stability.
2. **Unattenuated Shear Sensitivity:** Spatial higher-order derivatives near $\zeta = 0$ reduce purely to geometric grid derivatives (e.g., $Ri_{zzzz} \to \zeta_{zzzz} - 8\beta \zeta_z \zeta_{zzz} - 6\beta \zeta_{zz}^2$).
3. **Softening Noise Floor Requirement:** This unsuppressed sensitivity near neutrality provides the mathematical necessity for C-Z0HR's softening noise floor parameter $\epsilon_c Ri_c$ to avoid numerical grid ringing in atmospheric solvers.

### B. High-Stability Asymptotic Decay ($\zeta \gg 1$)

For strongly stable conditions ($\zeta \gg 1$), non-dimensional derivatives decay as:

$$R^{(n)}(\zeta) \approx (-1)^n n! \, \frac{\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h}{\beta_m^3} \zeta^{-(n+1)} \sim \mathcal{O}\left(\zeta^{-(n+1)}\right) \tag{8}$$

In physical vertical space (assuming constant local Obukhov length $L$):

$$\frac{d^n Ri}{dz^n} \approx (-1)^n n! \, \frac{L (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)}{\beta_m^3 z^{n+1}} \propto \frac{L}{z^{n+1}} \tag{9}$$

**NWP Biharmonic Operator Suppression:**
In atmospheric models incorporating 4th-order biharmonic diffusion ($-\nu_4 \nabla^4 Ri$), the $(1 + \beta \zeta)^{-5}$ decay in $R^{(4)}(\zeta)$ naturally damps physical high-order derivative noise aloft under strong stability. Consequently, persistent derivative spikes at $z \gg L$ indicate non-MOST physics (e.g., gravity wave breakdown or shear instability) rather than numerical artifacts of the similarity closure.
