In **Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST)** closures, non-dimensional atmospheric stability functions \(R(\zeta)\)—where \(\zeta = z/L\) is the stability parameter and \(L\) is the Obukhov length—are mapped into physical vertical space to evaluate spatial gradients of the **Gradient Richardson Number** (\(Ri=R(\zeta(z))\)) and flux profile functions.

---

### 1. The Partial Bell Matrix System (\(\mathbf{Ri}_z = \mathbf{B} \mathbf{R}_\zeta\))

Evaluating higher-order vertical spatial derivatives (\(Ri_z, Ri_{zz}, Ri_{zzz}, Ri_{zzzz}\)) is required for adaptive vertical grid diffusion, \(3\text{D}\) or vertical biharmonic hyperdiffusion operators (\(-\nu_4 \nabla^4 Ri\)), and numerical stability analysis near boundaries.

According to **Faà di Bruno's higher-order chain rule**, differentiating a composite function \(Ri(z) = R(\zeta(z))\) \(m\) times with respect to physical height \(z\) expands into a sum involving **partial Bell polynomials** \(B_{m,n}\):
\[\frac{d^m}{dz^m} R(\zeta(z)) = \sum_{n=1}^m R^{(n)}(\zeta) \cdot B_{m,n}\left(\zeta_z, \zeta_{zz}, \dots, \zeta_{(m-n+1)z}\right)\]

In matrix form, this lower-triangular transformation system connects non-dimensional similarity derivatives \(\mathbf{R}_\zeta\) to physical spatial derivatives \(\mathbf{Ri}_z\):
\[\mathbf{Ri}_z = \mathbf{B} \, \mathbf{R}_\zeta \implies \begin{bmatrix} Ri_z \ Ri_{zz} \ Ri_{zzz} \ Ri_{zzzz} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \zeta_z & 0 & 0 & 0 \ \zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^2 & 0 & 0 \ \zeta_{zzz} & 3\zeta_z\zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^3 & 0 \ \zeta_{zzzz} & 4\zeta_z\zeta_{zzz}+3\zeta_{zz}^2 & 6\zeta_z^2\zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R'(\zeta) \ R''(\zeta) \ R'''(\zeta) \ R^{(4)}(\zeta) \end{bmatrix}\]

---

### 2. Closure Mappings: Single-Coefficient vs. Dual-Coefficient

The physical spatial derivative profile depends directly on the mathematical form chosen for \(R(\zeta)\):

* **Single-Coefficient Businger–Dyer Closure:** Uses the rational form \(R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta}{1 + \beta \zeta}\), yielding closed-form physical spatial derivatives such as:
  \[Ri_{zz} = \frac{1}{(1 + \beta \zeta)^2} \left[ \zeta_{zz} - \frac{2\beta \zeta_z^2}{1 + \beta \zeta} \right]\]
* **Dual-Coefficient Monin–Obukhov Closure:** Accounts for distinct momentum (\(\beta_m\)) and scalar (\(\beta_h\)) expansion slopes with a neutral turbulent Prandtl number \(\alpha = \text{Pr}_t(0)\) via \(R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta(\alpha + \beta_h \zeta)}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^2}\). Evaluated at neutrality (\(\zeta = 0\)), derivative inputs yield:
  \[R'(0) = \alpha, \quad R''(0) = 2(\beta_h - 2\alpha \beta_m)\]

---

### 3. Asymptotic Regime Analysis & NWP Implications

Evaluating the transformation across stability limits reveals critical numerical behavior for Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) solvers:

#### A. Near-Neutral Limit (\(\zeta \to 0\))

* **Initial Curvature Spike:** Using standard empirical constants (\(\alpha = 1.0, \beta_m = 6.0, \beta_h = 5.0\)), the dual-coefficient closure gives \(R''(0) = 2(5 - 12) = -14\), compared to \(R''(0) = -10\) for single-coefficient closures with \(\beta = 5\). This represents a **~40% steeper initial curvature spike** near neutrality.
* **Unattenuated Shear Sensitivity:** Because prefactor damping terms approach \(1.0\) as \(\zeta \to 0\), physical spatial derivatives reduce purely to unattenuated geometric grid derivatives (e.g., \(\lim_{\zeta \to 0} Ri_{zzzz} = \zeta_{zzzz} + R''(0)(4\zeta_z \zeta_{zzz} + 3\zeta_{zz}^2) + \dots\)).
* **Noise Floor Justification:** High spatial derivative sensitivity near the surface on discrete, stretched vertical grids causes numerical grid-scale oscillations ("ringing"). This provides the explicit mathematical proof for why boundary-layer parameterizations require a **softening noise floor parameter** (\(\epsilon_c Ri_c > 0\)) to bound derivative growth near neutral stability.

#### B. High-Stability Limit (\(\zeta \gg 1\))

* **Asymptotic Decay:** Non-dimensional derivatives decay as \(R^{(n)}(\zeta) \approx (-1)^n n! \frac{\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h}{\beta_m^3} \zeta^{-(n+1)} \sim \mathcal{O}(\zeta^{-(n+1)})\). Assuming a constant local Obukhov length \(L\), physical vertical derivatives decay rapidly as:
  \[\frac{d^n Ri}{dz^n} \propto \frac{L}{z^{n+1}} \quad \left(\text{e.g., } Ri_{zzzz} \propto z^{-5}\right)\]
* **Natural Noise Suppression:** Because MOST closures naturally suppress physical higher-order spatial variations aloft under strong stability, fourth-order biharmonic hyperdiffusion operators (\(-\nu_4 \nabla^4 Ri\)) naturally damp computational noise aloft without distorting physical boundary-layer dynamics. Persistent derivative spikes aloft signal non-MOST physical phenomena (e.g., gravity wave breakdown or shear instability) or boundary reflections rather than similarity closure instability.

---

### 4. Observational Calibration via the Inverse Matrix (\(\mathbf{B}^{-1}\))

Because \(\mathbf{B}\) is a lower-triangular matrix with non-zero diagonal entries \(B_{k,k} = \zeta_z^k\), it is strictly invertible with determinant \(\det(\mathbf{B}) = \zeta_z^{10}\). The inverse mapping \(\mathbf{R}_\zeta = \mathbf{B}^{-1} \mathbf{Ri}_z\) allows observational tower or Large-Eddy Simulation (LES) profile data of physical spatial gradients (\(Ri_z, Ri_{zz}\)) to directly calibrate closure parameters (\(\alpha, \beta_m, \beta_h\)) near neutrality without assuming specific functional profiles _a priori_.
