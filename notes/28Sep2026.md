# Technical Report: Mathematical Mechanics and Coordinate Singularities in Monin–Obukhov Boundary Layer Profiling

## Executive Introduction

As night falls over flat prairie terrain, the surface cools by longwave radiation, decoupling the boundary layer and setting off an inertial oscillation aloft. This synoptic process spins up a nocturnal Low-Level Jet (LLJ), driving sharp momentum gradients directly above a shallow surface inversion.

For atmospheric modelers examining vertical tower profiles from field campaigns like CASES-99 or GABLS3, this environment produces two strikingly similar yet mathematically distinct profile phenomena: the Nose and the Knee.

### The Nose: A Kinematic Momentum Maximum

The **"Nose"** of a Low-Level Jet is a pure fluid-dynamical feature. It marks the altitude ($z_{\text{LLJ}}$) of peak horizontal wind speed, where mean vertical shear vanishes:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}U}{\mathrm{d}z} = 0$$

Because vertical shear appears squared in the denominator of the Gradient Richardson Number:

$$Ri = \frac{g}{\theta_0} \frac{\partial \theta / \partial z}{(\partial U / \partial z)^2}$$

approaching zero shear near the jet axis causes raw point-gradient $Ri$ values to spike or diverge. The nose is a genuine physical extremum in horizontal momentum transport.

### The Knee: A 1D Coordinate Fold Singularity

To understand the **"Knee,"** Generalized Similarity Profile Theory (GSPT) projects state-space dynamics onto the non-dimensional Obukhov coordinate $\zeta(z) = \frac{z}{L(z)}$. In a real stable boundary layer with height-varying heat and momentum fluxes, the Obukhov length scale varies dynamically with height ($L = L(z)$).

Differentiating $\zeta(z)$ with respect to physical height reveals how vertical flux divergence ($L' = \mathrm{d}L/\mathrm{d}z$) stretches or compresses similarity space:

$$\zeta_z = \frac{\mathrm{d}\zeta}{\mathrm{d}z} = \frac{1 - \zeta L'}{L}$$

Near the jet boundary, rapid variations in flux divergence force local coordinate compression. Where local flux divergence perfectly balances similarity scaling ($\zeta L' = 1$), the coordinate transformation derivative vanishes:

$$\zeta_z = 0$$

At this singular coordinate boundary—the Knee—the 1D mapping $z \mapsto \zeta(z)$ suffers a loss of local invertibility. Applying the total derivative rule to $Ri(\zeta(z))$ shows what happens to observed vertical curvature:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}^2 Ri}{\mathrm{d}z^2} = \underbrace{Ri_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2}_{\text{Intrinsic Stability Geometry}} + \underbrace{Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz}}_{\text{Flux-Coordinate Geometry}}$$

When $\zeta_z = 0$, the first physical gradient collapses ($Ri_z = Ri_\zeta \zeta_z = 0$), and the intrinsic thermodynamic stability term ($Ri_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2$) disappears completely. Physical curvature reduces purely to coordinate geometry ($Ri_{zz} = Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz}$), creating a flattening "knee" profile.

### The Interplay: Unmasking the Artifact

In tall-tower data (such as the 200 m Cabauw mast in GABLS3 or the 55 m CASES-99 array), a GSPT coordinate fold knee (e.g., $z \approx 45\text{ m}$) frequently develops near or just beneath the LLJ nose (e.g., $z \approx 60\text{ m}$).

Evaluating the diagnostic ratio of coordinate-to-intrinsic curvature:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{coord}} = \frac{Ri_\zeta \zeta_{zz}}{Ri_{\zeta\zeta} \zeta_z^2}$$

reveals that as $\zeta_z \to 0$, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{coord}}$ spikes from near unity to over $+170$. Over **99.4% of total observed profile bending** at the knee is driven purely by coordinate compression ($\zeta_{zz}$) rather than intrinsic thermodynamic stability physics ($Ri_{\zeta\zeta}$).

The Nose is a real kinematic boundary of maximum momentum, whereas the Knee is a mathematical projection artifact born from 1D scalar coordinate uninvertibility. Recognizing this distinction prevents modelers from misinterpreting a scalar coordinate fold singularity as a physical breakdown of turbulence.

## 1. Analytical Formulations for Monin–Obukhov Stability Closures

To systematically analyze higher-order spatial variations in numerical models, non-dimensional similarity functions $R(\zeta)$ must be evaluated at arbitrary derivative order $m$.

### A. Single-Coefficient Businger–Dyer Closure

For the rational single-coefficient closure $R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta}{1 + \beta \zeta} = \frac{1}{\beta}\left(1 - \frac{1}{1 + \beta \zeta}\right)$, the exact $m$-th derivative with respect to dimensionless height $\zeta$ for all $m \ge 1$ is:

$$R^{(m)}(\zeta) = (-1)^{m-1} m! \, \beta^{m-1} (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-(m+1)}$$

Evaluating the first four orders explicitly yields:

* **$m = 1$:** $R'(\zeta) = (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-2}$
* **$m = 2$:** $R''(\zeta) = -2\beta (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-3}$
* **$m = 3$:** $R'''(\zeta) = 6\beta^2 (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-4}$
* **$m = 4$:** $R^{(4)}(\zeta) = -24\beta^3 (1 + \beta \zeta)^{-5}$

### B. Dual-Coefficient Monin–Obukhov Closure

For the dual-coefficient closure $R(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta(\alpha + \beta_h \zeta)}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^2}$ (where $\alpha = \mathrm{Pr}_t(0)$ is the neutral turbulent Prandtl number and $\beta_h \neq \beta_m$), taking repeated derivatives yields the non-dimensional derivative vector $\mathbf{R}_\zeta$:

$$\mathbf{R}_\zeta = \begin{bmatrix} R'(\zeta) \\ R''(\zeta) \\ R'''(\zeta) \\ R^{(4)}(\zeta) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}  \frac{\alpha + (2\beta_h - \alpha \beta_m)\zeta}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^3} \\  \frac{2 \left[ \beta_m (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)\zeta + (\beta_h - 2\alpha \beta_m) \right]}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^4} \\  \frac{-6 \left[ \beta_m^2 (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)\zeta + \beta_m (2\beta_h - 3\alpha \beta_m) \right]}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^5} \\  \frac{24 \left[ \beta_m^3 (\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h)\zeta + \beta_m^2 (3\beta_h - 4\alpha \beta_m) \right]}{(1 + \beta_m \zeta)^6}  \end{bmatrix}$$

## 2. General Analytical Representations for Arbitrary Derivative Order $m$

Defining the logarithmic derivative as $f(\zeta) = \frac{R'(\zeta)}{R(\zeta)} = \frac{1}{\zeta} + \frac{\beta_h}{\alpha + \beta_h \zeta} - \frac{2\beta_m}{1 + \beta_m \zeta}$, arbitrary derivatives $R^{(m)}(\zeta)$ can be generated through three distinct mathematical frameworks:

### Faà di Bruno’s Formula (Complete Bell Polynomials)

Expressing $R(\zeta) = C e^{F(\zeta)}$ where $F'(\zeta) = f(\zeta)$, the $m$-th derivative is generated by the complete exponential Bell polynomial $Y_m$:

$$R^{(m)}(\zeta) = R(\zeta) \cdot Y_m\Big(f(\zeta),\, f'(\zeta),\, f''(\zeta),\, \dots,\, f^{(m-1)}(\zeta)\Big)$$

where the component derivative vector is given by:

$$f^{(k)}(\zeta) = (-1)^k k! \left[ \frac{1}{\zeta^{k+1}} + \frac{\beta_h^{k+1}}{(\alpha + \beta_h\zeta)^{k+1}} - \frac{2\beta_m^{k+1}}{(1 + \beta_m\zeta)^{k+1}} \right]$$

### Leibniz Recurrence Relation (Discrete Convolution)

Differentiating $R'(\zeta) = f(\zeta) R(\zeta)$ $m$ times yields a discrete convolution recurrence ideal for algorithmic code implementation:

$$R^{(m+1)}(\zeta) = \sum_{k=0}^m \binom{m}{k} f^{(k)}(\zeta) R^{(m-k)}(\zeta)$$

### Collapsed 3-Fold Cauchy Convolution

Decomposing $R(\zeta) = C \cdot R_1(\zeta) R_2(\zeta) R_3(\zeta)$ into linear factors $R_1(\zeta) = \zeta$, $R_2(\zeta) = \alpha + \beta_h\zeta$, and rational factor $R_3(\zeta) = (1 + \beta_m\zeta)^{-2}$, all higher derivatives of $R_1$ and $R_2$ vanish ($R_1^{(i)} = R_2^{(j)} = 0$ for $i, j \ge 2$).

Consequently, for any order $m \ge 2$, the 3-fold Cauchy convolution collapses to exactly **4 non-zero terms**:

$$R^{(m)}(\zeta) = C \left[ R_1 R_2 R_3^{(m)} + m \left( \beta_h R_1 + R_2 \right) R_3^{(m-1)} + m(m-1) \beta_h R_3^{(m-2)} \right]$$

where:

$$R_3^{(k)}(\zeta) = (-1)^k (k+1)! \frac{\beta_m^k}{(1 + \beta_m\zeta)^{k+2}}$$

## 3. Asymptotic Scaling & Numerical Solver Stability

### High-Stability Limit ($\zeta \gg 1$)

In strongly stable atmospheric regimes ($\zeta \to \infty$), higher-order non-dimensional derivatives decay as a power law:

$$R^{(m)}(\zeta) \approx (-1)^m m! \, \frac{\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h}{\beta_m^3} \zeta^{-(m+1)} \sim \mathcal{O}\left(\zeta^{-(m+1)}\right)$$

In physical space across vertical height $z$ (assuming a constant local Obukhov length scale $L$), spatial derivatives scale as:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}^m Ri}{\mathrm{d}z^m} \propto \frac{L}{z^{m+1}}$$

This rapid power-law decay demonstrates that standard Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) closures naturally suppress physical higher-order variations aloft. As a consequence, 3D biharmonic hyperdiffusion operators ($-\nu_4 \nabla^4 Ri$) applied in Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) models damp high-wavenumber grid noise aloft without distorting resolved physical atmospheric stratification.

### Near-Neutral Limit ($\zeta \to 0$)

At neutral stability ($\zeta = 0$), prefactor terms evaluate to exact empirical parameter constants:

| Derivative Order | Single-Coefficient Closure ($\beta = 5.0$) | Dual-Coefficient Closure ($\alpha=1.0, \beta_m=6.0, \beta_h=5.0$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **$R'(0)$** | $1.0$ | $\alpha = 1.0$ |
| **$R''(0)$** | $-2\beta = -10.0$ | $2(\beta_h - 2\alpha \beta_m) = -14.0$ |
| **$R'''(0)$** | $6\beta^2 = +150.0$ | $6\beta_m (3\alpha \beta_m - 2\beta_h) = +288.0$ |
| **$R^{(4)}(0)$** | $-24\beta^3 = -3000.0$ | $-24\beta_m^2 (4\alpha \beta_m - 3\beta_h) = -7344.0$ |

#### Initial Curvature Spike

Using standard empirical parameters, the dual-coefficient closure produces $R''(0) = -14.0$ compared to $R''(0) = -10.0$ for single-coefficient closures—a **40% steeper initial curvature spike** near neutrality. Unattenuated physical spatial derivatives near $\zeta \to 0$ maximize sensitivity to geometric grid variations, establishing the mathematical necessity for softening noise floors ($\epsilon_c Ri_c > 0$) in non-hydrostatic NWP solvers.

## 4. Physical Spatial Mapping via Partial Bell Polynomial Matrices

### General Transformation

The non-dimensional derivative vector $\mathbf{R}_\zeta$ maps directly into physical vertical spatial derivatives $\mathbf{Ri}_z = \left[\frac{\mathrm{d}Ri}{\mathrm{d}z}, \frac{\mathrm{d}^2Ri}{\mathrm{d}z^2}, \frac{\mathrm{d}^3Ri}{\mathrm{d}z^3}, \frac{\mathrm{d}^4Ri}{\mathrm{d}z^4}\right]^T$ via the $4 \times 4$ lower-triangular partial Bell matrix $\mathbf{B}$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} Ri_z \\ Ri_{zz} \\ Ri_{zzz} \\ Ri_{zzzz} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \zeta_z & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^2 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zzz} & 3\zeta_z\zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^3 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zzzz} & 4\zeta_z\zeta_{zzz} + 3\zeta_{zz}^2 & 6\zeta_z^2\zeta_{zz} & \zeta_z^4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R'(\zeta) \\ R''(\zeta) \\ R'''(\zeta) \\ R^{(4)}(\zeta) \end{bmatrix}$$

where $B_{m,n}$ are partial exponential Bell polynomials generated recursively by $B_{m+1, n} = \zeta_z B_{m, n-1} + \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z} B_{m, n}$,  with $B_{1,1} = \zeta_z$.

### Matrix Structural Collapse at the Coordinate Fold ($\zeta_z = 0$)

When vertical flux divergence balances similarity scaling ($\zeta_z = 0$), the partial Bell matrix $\mathbf{B}$ collapses into a highly sparse, rank-deficient structure:

$$\mathbf{B}\Big\vert{}_{\zeta_z = 0} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zz} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zzz} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \zeta_{zzzz} & 3\zeta_{zz}^2 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Multiplying $\mathbf{B}\big\vert{}_{\zeta_z = 0}$ by the non-dimensional derivative vector $\mathbf{R}_\zeta$ yields the physical spatial derivative vector:

$$\begin{bmatrix} Ri_z \\ Ri_{zz} \\ Ri_{zzz} \\ Ri_{zzzz} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \zeta_{zz} R'(\zeta) \\ \zeta_{zzz} R'(\zeta) \\ \zeta_{zzzz} R'(\zeta) + 3\zeta_{zz}^2 R''(\zeta) \end{bmatrix}$$

### Physical and Numerical Mechanics at the Fold

1. **Singularity & Main Diagonal Collapse:** Because all diagonal entries $B_{k,k} = \zeta_z^k$ vanish, $\det(\mathbf{B}) = \zeta_z^{10} = 0$. The mapping $z \mapsto \zeta(z)$ loses local invertibility, creating a 1D coordinate fold singularity.
2. **Higher-Order Column Erasure:** Columns 3 and 4 collapse entirely to zero. Observed physical vertical derivatives up to order 4 become completely independent of similarity derivatives higher than $R''(\zeta)$.
3. **Physical Gradient Flattening ($Ri_z = 0$):** The first physical spatial derivative vanishes identically, confirming a stationary point (profile plateau or "knee") in physical height space.
4. **Pure Coordinate Curvature ($Ri_{zz} = R'(\zeta) \zeta_{zz}$):** Intrinsic thermodynamic stability curvature ($R''(\zeta)\zeta_z^2$) vanishes completely. Physical profile bending is governed entirely by the geometric acceleration/stretching of the similarity coordinate ($\zeta_{zz}$).
5. **Biharmonic Hyperdiffusion Reactivation at Order 4:** At derivative order 4, the entry $B_{4,2} = 3\zeta_{zz}^2$ yields the non-zero coupling term $3 R''(\zeta) \zeta_{zz}^2$. Biharmonic hyperdiffusion filters ($-\nu_4 \nabla^4 Ri$) in atmospheric solvers will react to this term, applying numerical damping near the GSPT fold to maintain solver stability without distorting true physical turbulence.
