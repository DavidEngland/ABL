**Why Arctic temperatures are rising twice as fast as the rest of the planet — and what atmospheric models are missing**

The Arctic is warming at an alarming rate — **twice the global average**. This "Arctic Amplification" threatens sea ice, disrupts weather patterns, and accelerates global climate change.

But here's the problem: our best climate models still struggle to get the Arctic right, especially during the dark polar winter. Why?

**It comes down to the first 100 meters above the ice.**

When the sun sets for months, a stable air layer forms near the surface — cold, stratified, and barely mixing. Most climate models use vertical grids that are too coarse to capture this delicate structure. They blur out the sharp temperature gradients, predict too much mixing, and end up **underestimating how cold (and stable) the Arctic surface really gets**.

This isn't just an academic problem. Wrong near-surface temperatures mean:
- ❄️ Incorrect sea ice melt timing
- 🌡️ Biased projections of Arctic warming rates

As we refine Arctic climate models, every degree of accuracy matters. Better boundary layer physics → better sea ice forecasts → better long-term climate projections → better-informed policy decisions.

**Climate models are only as good as their weakest layer — and that layer is often the one closest to the ground.**
